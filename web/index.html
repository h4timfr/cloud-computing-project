    Get flood alerts by email

    Receive an email when FloodPulse detects WARNING or CRITICAL flood risk.

    You’ll receive a confirmation email from Amazon Web Services. Your subscription becomes active only after you confirm it.

    FloodPulse does not send marketing email. Email addresses are used only for this SNS notification subscription: Amazon SNS holds the address, and FloodPulse itself keeps no address at all — just a count that limits how often one address can ask.

    Amazon SNS includes its own unsubscribe link in the alert emails it sends for this topic, so you can stop them from the email itself. FloodPulse has no unsubscribe page of its own.

    City selection

    FloodPulse monitors a selected fleet of coastal cities. A city is in the fleet because a published source documents a coastal, rainfall or cyclone hazard for it; each city's own page states its reason.

    This is a selected monitoring fleet, not complete national coverage: many flood-exposed places in India are not monitored here, and a city's absence says nothing about its risk. Cities are listed in coast order, not ranked. Selection is fixed documentation, separate from the live risk score, which moves with today's weather and is not an official warning.

    Sources used to select the fleet:

    • MoEF, Final Frontier (2009). moef.gov.in
    • IMD, Rainfall Extremes over Coastal Stations. rsmcnewdelhi.imd.gov.in
    • INCOIS, About Storm Surge. tsunami.incois.gov.in
    • World Bank, Surat Resilience Enhancement Project (P177799). documents1.worldbank.org
    • ThinkHazard! (GFDRR / World Bank). thinkhazard.org

      About

      What FloodPulse is

      FloodPulse Coastal India is an academic cloud-computing prototype focused on selected coastal urban flood-risk locations. It watches rainfall and ground conditions for the cities in its fleet and shows how close each one is to flooding. It monitors cities individually, not states, coasts or the country, and every number on the site is one of three things: measured, derived from measurements, or simulated.

      Why it exists

      Urban flash flooding is decided by small things adding up: how hard it is raining, how wet the ground already is, and whether the drains have any room left. Those signals exist, but they are scattered across different systems. FloodPulse puts them on one page, in plain words, and keeps the reasoning visible.

      Each city is scored independently from its own coordinates and its own thresholds.

      How it works

      1. Collect. Rainfall and soil moisture are fetched from Open-Meteo for each city; drainage, water level and flow are produced by a simulator, because this project has no physical sensors.
      2. Score. The five values are combined into one risk score between 0 and 1 by the deterministic heuristic-v1 formula, then filtered so a single noisy reading cannot move a city's risk band on its own.
      3. Decide. A city at WARNING or CRITICAL opens an alert record, which closes again once the city is back to NORMAL. Repeats are rate-limited per city.
      4. Show. This page reads the API every few seconds and labels every number with where it came from.

      Built on

      • AWS Lambda — one function that ingests readings, scores them and answers the API
      • API Gateway — the public HTTP endpoint this page reads
      • DynamoDB — one table holding current values, history, incidents and the weather cache
      • S3 — this static dashboard
      • SNS — the notification topic alerts are published to
      • Open-Meteo — the public weather provider behind the real measurements

      Everything runs on managed services with no servers to keep alive, which is also why the whole project fits inside a student budget.

      Flood safety guidance

      FloodPulse tells you where flood risk is rising. It does not issue official warnings and it is not an authority. If a flood is happening or forecast, follow your local government and disaster-management authorities — they know your street, your drains and your evacuation routes.

      • Do not walk or drive through floodwater. Moving water is stronger than it looks and the road surface may be gone.
      • Move to higher ground when told to, and stay there until officials say it is safe to return.
      • Keep away from drains, canals and fast-moving water, including water you can see the bottom of.
      • Follow instructions from local authorities over anything this page says.
      • Watch official weather and disaster-management channels for warnings and evacuation orders.
      • Never enter a flooded building or basement until it has been declared safe — water and electricity do not mix.
      • Check on neighbours who may need help getting out, once you are safe yourself.

      Limitations, stated plainly

        Overview Cities Alerts Data & Methodology About & safety guidance

        FloodPulse is a university cloud-computing project. Risk scores are deterministic heuristic-v1 values, clearly labelled as such and never presented as a prediction. Every metric carries a provenance tag: REAL comes from the external weather provider, MODEL is derived from those real fields, and SIM is generated by the FloodPulse simulator because no real sensor exists for it.

        heuristic-v1 is a hand-tuned deterministic scoring heuristic over sensor values. It is not a calibrated hydrological model, not a statistical forecast and not a machine-learning prediction, and it is not an official flood warning. For official emergency guidance, follow local government and disaster-management authorities.
